IM channels let your team talk to Octop agents through the chat tools they already use every day. When someone sends a message on Feishu, WeCom, DingTalk, or Telegram, Octop routes it to the linked agent and replies in the same thread — no need to open the web UI. Each channel belongs to exactly one agent, so you control which agent handles which platform.

## Where to configure channels

Channels are configured per agent:

```text
Personalization → Channels
```

Select the agent at the top of the page, then toggle on the platform you want and enter its credentials.

## Platform setup

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Feishu">
    Create a Feishu app in the [Feishu Open Platform](https://open.feishu.cn/) and note down the **App ID** and **App Secret**.

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Enable the channel">
        Toggle on **Feishu** under **Personalization → Channels**.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Enter your app credentials">
        Fill in the **App ID** and **App Secret** from your Feishu app settings.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Register the webhook">
        Copy the webhook URL shown by Octop and paste it into the **Event subscription** section of your Feishu app configuration, then save the Feishu app settings.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Verify">
        Send a message to the bot to confirm the channel is receiving events.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    Octop also ships a guided terminal wizard that walks through the same flow:

    ```bash
    octop channel feishu-setup
    ```
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="WeCom">
    Set up a WeCom (企业微信) application and gather the **Corp ID** (企业ID), **Agent ID**, and **Secret**.

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Enable the channel">
        Toggle on **WeCom** under **Personalization → Channels**.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Enter your credentials">
        Fill in the Corp ID, Agent ID, and Secret from your WeCom application settings.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Configure the callback URL">
        Add the callback URL Octop shows to your WeCom app so it can receive messages.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    For a quicker bind using a QR-code scan, run `octop channel bind` and scan the code with the WeCom mobile app.
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="DingTalk">
    Create a DingTalk bot in the [DingTalk Open Platform](https://open.dingtalk.com/) and collect the **Client ID** and **Client Secret**.

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Enable the channel">
        Toggle on **DingTalk** under **Personalization → Channels**.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Enter your bot credentials">
        Fill in the Client ID and Client Secret from your DingTalk bot configuration.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Configure the webhook">
        Copy the callback URL provided by Octop into the **Message receive mode** settings of your DingTalk app, enable the relevant event scopes, and save.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="QQ">
    Register an application on the QQ bot open platform and obtain the **App ID** and **Token**.

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Enable the channel">
        Toggle on **QQ** under **Personalization → Channels**.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Enter your bot credentials">
        Fill in the App ID and Token, then save.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Telegram">
    Create a bot with [@BotFather](https://t.me/BotFather) and copy the **Bot Token**.

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Enable the channel">
        Toggle on **Telegram** under **Personalization → Channels**.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Enter the bot token">
        Paste the Bot Token and save. Octop connects through the Telegram Bot API and supports both direct and group messages.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    <Warning>
      Keep your Bot Token secret. If it is ever exposed, revoke it in BotFather and update the channel in Octop.
    </Warning>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Others">
    Octop also ships channels for **WeChat** (personal WeChat via an iLink Bot), **Yuanbao** (Tencent Yuanbao conversations), **Xiaoyi** (Huawei Xiaoyi / OpenClaw over the A2A protocol), and **MQTT** (routing IoT device or robot messages to an agent).

    Each follows the same pattern: toggle the channel on under **Personalization → Channels**, then fill in the credentials that platform requires.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<Tip>
  To manage channels from the terminal (`list`, `test`, `delete`, `feishu-setup`, `bind`), see [Channel & cron commands](/cli/channel-cron-commands#octop-channel).
</Tip>

<Note>
  Each agent can have multiple channels active at the same time. All messages are processed by the same underlying agent, so the conversation context stays unified regardless of which platform a user messages from.
</Note>
